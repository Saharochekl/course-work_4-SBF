\date{Draft of 29 July 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We present a direct empirical calibration of surface brightness fluctuation
(SBF) magnitudes measured with JWST/NIRCam in F150W.  The sample comprises the
14 early-type galaxies observed in JWST program GO-3055 for the
TRGB--SBF distance-ladder project.  We follow the analysis geometry of
Jensen et al. (2025, arXiv:2502.15935), measuring SBF and color in two
concentric circular annuli spanning 8.2--16.4 and 16.4--32.8 arcsec and
combining the two measurements with inverse-variance weights.  F090W--F150W
colors are measured in the same annuli as the SBF signal.  Absolute
fluctuation magnitudes are anchored by the revised, color-dependent JWST TRGB
distances of Chazov et al. (2026, arXiv:2603.11160).  For the full sample we
obtain the preliminary relation
\[
 \Mbar_{150}=(-3.173\pm0.023)
 +(0.666^{+0.423}_{-0.476})[C_{090-150}-0.5907],
\]
with an inferred intrinsic scatter of 0.069 mag.  Leave-one-out
cross-validation against TRGB gives a mean residual of $+0.008$ mag and an
RMS of 0.099 mag, corresponding to approximately 4.7 per cent in distance.
A color-independent model is statistically competitive
($\Delta{\rm AICc}=1.71$ relative to the linear relation), so the present
sample does not yet provide decisive evidence for the color slope.
Measurements in the inner and outer annuli show materially different trends,
including a highly significant 0.168 mag radial difference in NGC 3379 despite
an exceptionally uniform residual image.  We discuss the implications for
stellar-population corrections and for transferring the TRGB zero point to
future JWST SBF samples.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Surface brightness fluctuations arise from Poisson variations in the number
and luminosity of unresolved stars within each resolution element
\cite{TonrySchneider1988}.  For old stellar populations the near-infrared SBF
signal is strong, allowing efficient distance measurements to early-type
galaxies well beyond the reach of resolved-star methods.  Its absolute
amplitude, however, depends on the luminosity function of the evolved stellar
population and therefore must be calibrated as a function of a population
proxy, normally an integrated optical or near-infrared color.

The TRGB--SBF project is constructing a Population-II distance ladder in
which a geometrically calibrated tip of the red giant branch (TRGB) zero point
is transferred to SBF measurements.  Jensen et al. \cite{Jensen2025}
(hereafter Paper III) used JWST TRGB distances for the GO-3055 galaxies to
recalibrate the existing HST/WFC3-IR F110W SBF scale.  Their analysis retained
the established HST SBF measurements and used DECam or Pan-STARRS $g-z$
colors.  With cluster-mean TRGB distances for Virgo and Fornax, they obtained
\[
 \Mbar_{110}=(1.86\pm0.16)[(g-z)_{\rm PS}-1.30]
             -(2.760\pm0.016).
\]
The revised calibration produced
$H_0=73.8\pm0.7\ ({\rm stat})\pm2.3\ ({\rm sys})$
\si{km.s^{-1}.Mpc^{-1}}.

The present study takes the next direct step: both the fluctuation signal and
the population color are measured from the JWST GO-3055 images themselves.
F150W provides the SBF signal and F090W--F150W the color.  The TRGB anchors are
the thin-slice distance moduli in Chazov et al. \cite{Chazov2026}
(hereafter Paper IV), who calibrated the color dependence of the F090W TRGB
using the NGC 4258 maser distance.  Paper IV found
$M_{\rm F090W}^{\rm TRGB}=-4.398\pm0.017$ mag for
$(F090W-F150W)_0<1.65$ mag, followed by a polynomial decline at redder color.
Their thin-slice distances are on average $0.028\pm0.008$ mag shorter than
the earlier literature values derived from the same imaging.  That revision
must be kept explicit when comparing our F150W zero point with Paper III.

Our goals are to (1) reproduce the Paper III annular measurement geometry,
(2) establish a direct JWST F150W SBF calibration, (3) cross-validate SBF
distances against the independent TRGB measurements, and (4) test whether
radial population variations and host-galaxy luminosity explain part of the
remaining scatter.

\section{Data}
\subsection{GO-3055 sample}

The sample contains 14 galaxies: three in the Fornax cluster, nine in the
Virgo region, and two in nearby groups.  Each target has NIRCam F090W and
F150W imaging.  F150W is used for the fluctuation measurement and F090W is
combined with it to form the population-sensitive color
$C_{090-150}=F090W-F150W$.  The adopted TRGB distance moduli are the
thin-slice values from Paper IV.  These distances are tied to the
NGC 4258 maser modulus of 29.397 mag.

\section{SBF analysis}

\subsection{Galaxy model, masks, and residual image}

A scalar sky level is estimated outside the science annuli and subtracted from
the F150W image.  A first mask removes invalid pixels, detector gaps, foreground
objects, and compact sources.  Isophotes are fitted to the usable galaxy light
and interpolated to form a full-frame smooth model.  Detector gaps are filled
only for the purpose of obtaining a stable isophotal solution; they do not
contribute to the SBF measurement.

The smooth model is subtracted from the background-corrected image.  Compact
sources detected in the residual image are then masked, and the residual is
normalized by the square root of the smooth galaxy model.  A symmetric
$3.5\sigma$ clipping of extreme residual pixels is retained as a deliberate
modification to the historical pipeline.  Its effect on the final zero point
must be reported as a sensitivity test rather than silently absorbed into the
method.

\subsection{Power spectrum and unresolved sources}

For each annulus the azimuthally averaged residual power spectrum is fitted
over the adopted frequency interval $0.04\leq k\leq0.25$ using
\[
 P(k)=P_0 E(k)+P_1 .
\]
Here $E(k)$ is the expectation power spectrum obtained from the PSF convolved
with the annular mask/window, $P_0$ is the fitted amplitude of the
PSF-shaped component, and $P_1$ is a constant white-noise term.  The
fluctuation power attributed to the stellar population is
\[
 P_{\rm SBF}=P_0-P_r,
\]
where $P_r$ is the estimated contribution of unresolved globular clusters and
background galaxies below the masking threshold.  The apparent fluctuation
magnitude follows from the photometric zero point and the mean galaxy model
level in the annulus.

All current annular fits return negative $P_1$.  Because this behavior is
nearly universal rather than confined to individual galaxies, it is treated
as evidence that the constant-noise component is an imperfect description of
the correlated noise in the drizzled images, not as an automatic rejection of
all $P_0$ estimates.  A final paper must quantify the sensitivity to the
adopted frequency window and to a noise-template extension of the model.

\subsection{Matched colors and uncertainties}

F090W is PSF-matched and registered to F150W, and the integrated color is
measured in exactly the same usable pixels and annuli as the SBF signal.  This
implements the central warning in Paper III that colors measured in the
high-signal galaxy core can be biased relative to the regions used for SBF.
The two ring measurements are retained separately and are also combined with
inverse-variance weights.

The present internal error includes the fitting uncertainty, stability across
the adopted $k_{\min}$ choices, the difference between the two annuli, PSF
scatter, and a working uncertainty in $P_r$.  Foreground-extinction,
large-scale sky, and common TRGB zero-point uncertainties are not yet complete;
the quoted error bars must therefore not be described as the final total error
budget.

\section{Results}
\subsection{F150W calibration}

Using all 14 individual Paper IV TRGB distances, the preliminary linear
calibration at the median color $C_0=0.5907$ is
\begin{equation}
 \Mbar_{150}=(-3.1726)
 +(0.6656)(C_{090-150}-0.5907),
 \label{eq:f150-calibration}
\end{equation}
with an intrinsic scatter of 0.0695 mag.  Bootstrap 16th--84th percentiles are
$[-3.193,-3.147]$ mag for the intercept and $[0.190,1.089]$ for the slope.
The broad slope interval is a direct consequence of the narrow color range and
the small sample.

\centering
\paperfigure{../../runs/sbf2_go3055/analysis/figures/go3055_f150w_sbf_calibration.png}{0.78\textwidth}
\caption{Direct JWST F150W SBF calibration for GO-3055.  This is the JWST
counterpart of the HST F110W calibration shown in Fig.~2 of Paper III.
The vertical axis is inverted so that brighter SBF is upward.  Before
submission the secondary ``clean'' fit must use the residual-structure flag
rather than radial annulus disagreement.}
\label{fig:calibration}

A constant absolute SBF magnitude has
${\rm AICc}=-22.83$, compared with $-21.11$ for the linear relation.
The difference $\Delta{\rm AICc}=1.71$ is not decisive, while leave-one-out
RMS values are 0.0984 and 0.0987 mag respectively.  Quadratic and broken-line
fits reduce the raw RMS slightly but are disfavored after accounting for model
complexity.  We therefore report the linear calibration for direct comparison
with Paper III but do not claim a secure detection of its color slope.

\subsection{Observed color correlations}

Paper III found a well-constrained F110W slope after enlarging the
calibration set with cluster-mean distances and a broader color baseline.  We
compare unweighted Pearson and Spearman coefficients for our 14 objects, our
measurements of the eight objects overlapping Table~1 of Paper III, and all
14 Paper III calibration points.  For Paper III we compute
$\Mbar_{110}^{\rm obs}=\mbar_{110}-\mu_{\rm cluster}$ rather than correlating
color with a magnitude already produced by their published color law.
For our full sample, the unweighted coefficients are
$r=0.30$ ($p=0.297$) and $\rho=0.24$ ($p=0.401$).  Restricting our data to
the eight Paper-III-overlap galaxies gives $r=0.49$ ($p=0.220$), whereas the
14 observed Paper III calibration points give $r=0.97$
($p<10^{-8}$) and $\rho=0.96$.  Thus the current F150W data do not show a
statistically significant raw color correlation, while the Paper III F110W
relation is strong over its broader color baseline and cluster-augmented
sample.  The two color indices themselves are nevertheless strongly correlated
for the eight common galaxies: F090W--F150W versus Pan-STARRS $g-z$ gives
$r=0.87$ ($p=0.005$) and $\rho=0.88$ ($p=0.004$).  The weak F150W
SBF--color relation therefore cannot be dismissed as a complete failure of
our infrared color to track the optical stellar-population sequence.

\subsection{Radial measurements}

The inner annuli give a slope of approximately 1.36 with intrinsic scatter
0.066 mag, whereas the outer annuli give a much weaker slope of approximately
0.28 and a larger scatter of 0.091 mag.  The two measurements for a galaxy are
correlated and must not be treated as 28 independent calibrators.  Their
difference is nevertheless a useful diagnostic of stellar-population
gradients and residual modeling systematics.

\begin{figure*}
\centering
\paperfigure{../../runs/sbf2_go3055/analysis/figures/go3055_annulus_local_color_sbf.png}{0.82\textwidth}
\caption{Annulus-matched local color and absolute F150W SBF magnitude.
Circles and squares denote the inner and outer annuli, respectively; grey
segments connect measurements of the same galaxy.  Unlike Paper III, which
reports the weighted annular means in its calibration table, we retain this
ring-by-ring view to test whether the population correction is radially
stable.}
\label{fig:annuli}
\end{figure*}

NGC 3379 has the most spatially uniform normalized residual in the sample, but
its annuli differ by 0.168 mag.  The outer annulus is bluer by approximately
0.031 mag and has fainter SBF, in the opposite direction and at much larger
amplitude than predicted by the global color relation.  The unresolved-source
correction is negligible in the inner annulus and below one per cent of $P_0$
in the outer annulus.  NGC 3379 is therefore retained as a high-quality
measurement and treated as evidence that annulus disagreement is not
equivalent to poor residual structure.

\subsection{Cross-validation against TRGB}

For each galaxy, the color relation is refitted after excluding that galaxy,
and the excluded SBF distance is compared with its Paper IV thin-slice TRGB
distance.  The mean residual is
\[
 \langle\mu_{\rm SBF,LOO}-\mu_{\rm TRGB}\rangle=+0.0077~{\rm mag},
\]
with RMS 0.0987 mag and median absolute residual 0.0919 mag.  The largest
absolute residuals occur for NGC 3379, NGC 4552, NGC 1399, NGC 4472, and
NGC 4649.

\subsection{Dependence on host-galaxy luminosity}

We test whether the F150W SBF magnitude correlates with the ordinary
aperture luminosity of the host.  The available quantity is the absolute
F150W magnitude of the smooth galaxy model inside 32.8 arcsec, not a total
galaxy magnitude.  We therefore present both the direct correlation and the
correlation of the residual about Eq.~\ref{eq:f150-calibration}.  The latter
tests whether host luminosity carries information beyond the integrated color.
The direct Pearson coefficient is $r=0.36$ ($p=0.203$).  After subtracting the
linear color relation it increases to $r=0.52$ ($p=0.056$), with Spearman
$\rho=0.50$ ($p=0.069$).  This is not a formal detection, but it is sufficiently
close to significance to motivate replacement of the fixed-aperture magnitude
with homogeneous total photometry or stellar-mass estimates.

The present work is deliberately close to Paper III in measurement geometry:
both use the same two annuli and their weighted mean, and both require the
color to correspond to the SBF region.  The comparison is not a literal
repeat, because NIRCam F150W and F090W--F150W respond differently to stellar
population age and metallicity than WFC3/IR F110W and optical $g-z$.

The distance-anchor treatment is also different.  Paper III shows that using
cluster means can reduce calibration scatter when individual measurement
uncertainties are comparable to cluster depth.  Our use of the individual
Paper IV thin-slice distances is the stricter leave-one-galaxy-out test, but it
also preserves line-of-sight depth and individual TRGB noise.  Before
submission, Eq.~\ref{eq:f150-calibration} must be recomputed using the exact
Paper III cluster-mean prescription.  The individual-distance and
cluster-mean results should be reported side by side rather than selecting the
one with smaller scatter after seeing the answer.

Paper IV introduces a second controlled difference: its updated thin-slice
moduli are on average $0.028\pm0.008$ mag below the earlier measurements.
Consequently, an F150W zero point calibrated with Paper IV cannot be compared
to the Paper III zero point without identifying the TRGB version.  The anchor
revision alone would shift an absolute SBF zero point at approximately the
same 0.03 mag level, even if the measured apparent SBF magnitudes were
unchanged.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Is a color term detected?}

The full-sample linear slope is positive and its bootstrap 16th percentile is
above zero.  This statement by itself is insufficient evidence for a physical
law: a constant model has slightly better AICc and essentially identical
leave-one-out performance.  The fitted slope is also sensitive to the
Virgo--Fornax balance, the radial annulus chosen, and the narrow color range.
The correct conclusion is therefore that a Paper-III-like linear law is
consistent with the data, but a color-independent F150W zero point is not
excluded.

A tentative Virgo--Fornax offset of approximately $-0.12$ mag is visible.
Although its bootstrap interval does not cross zero, adding the group step
worsens AICc by approximately 0.98 because only three Fornax galaxies are
available.  It should be presented as an exploratory population/environment
test, not as a detected second zero point.

\subsection{Radial behavior and residual structure}

The annular comparison is the most important diagnostic added beyond the
Paper III presentation.  A large annulus difference can occur in an
exceptionally clean residual image, while a visually structured residual can
have similar weighted SBF values in both annuli.  Quality control must
therefore separate model coverage, large-scale residual structure, and radial
SBF gradients.  NGC 4472, NGC 4486, NGC 4636, and NGC 4649 show the strongest
large-scale residual structure in the current sector-based diagnostic.
NGC 1380 and NGC 4697 have incomplete outer-annulus model coverage.

\subsection{Current limitations}

Three issues prevent the present draft from being submission-ready.
First, the total systematic error budget must include foreground extinction,
large-scale background uncertainty, the common NGC 4258/TRGB zero point, and
the effect of the Paper IV thin/thick choice.  Second, the universal negative
$P_1$ values require a controlled correlated-noise test, ideally using a
noise-template extension of the power-spectrum model.  Third, the
compact-source mask and $P_r$ completeness model require injection or
threshold sensitivity tests.  These tests should be reported as changes in
the final zero point and slope rather than as qualitative statements.

\section{Conclusions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item All 14 GO-3055 galaxies completed the F150W SBF measurement and artifact
verification in the two Paper III annuli; all currently retain review-level
quality flags, dominated by non-positive fitted $P_1$.
\item The preliminary full-sample calibration has zero point
$-3.173$ mag at $F090W-F150W=0.5907$, slope 0.666, and intrinsic scatter
0.069 mag.
\item Leave-one-out comparison with the revised Paper IV TRGB distances gives
an RMS of 0.099 mag, equivalent to approximately 4.7 per cent in distance,
with negligible mean bias.
\item The present sample does not decisively distinguish a linear color
correction from a constant F150W absolute fluctuation magnitude.
\item Inner and outer annuli do not follow the same empirical color trend.
NGC 3379 demonstrates that a significant radial SBF difference can coexist
with an exceptionally clean residual image.
\item A direct comparison with Paper III requires a second calibration using
their cluster-mean distance prescription and an explicit accounting of the
Paper IV TRGB zero-point revision.
\end{enumerate}
